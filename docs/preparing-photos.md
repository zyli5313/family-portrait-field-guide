# Preparing the photos

The source photo decides most of the result. This page covers what a usable photo looks like and how to get one from relatives who do not like being photographed.

## What a usable photo looks like

- **One face, large and sharp.** The face should fill a good part of the frame. A distant face inside a crowded group shot is not enough.
- **Even light.** Daylight from a window facing the person is ideal. Avoid strong light from behind.
- **Facing the camera.** A slight angle is fine; a profile is not.
- **No sunglasses, no heavy filters.** Ordinary glasses are fine if the person always wears them.
- **Recent.** A photo from this year, so children look their current age.

## Asking relatives

Send a short message with three requests: daylight, eye height, original file. Offer to take the picture yourself during the next video call if they prefer. For children, ask the parent to photograph them during an activity they enjoy and take a burst; one sharp frame is enough.

## Pets

Photograph the pet at its own eye level. A treat held just above the lens keeps it looking forward. Make sure the coat colour and markings are visible, because those are what the family will check first.

## Permission and privacy

Only use photos you have permission to use, and for children, the consent of a parent or guardian. Before uploading anything, read how the service stores photos. VibePico, for example, prepares photos in your browser first and states that uploaded copies expire after 24 hours; its [biometric policy](https://vibepico.com/biometric-policy) explains how faces are handled.
