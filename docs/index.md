# Separate Photos to One Family Portrait: A Field Guide

This short guide explains how to turn a set of separate photos, one per person, into a single family portrait. It is written for the person in the family who ends up organising everything: collecting pictures, chasing relatives, and deciding who stands where.

The guide has three parts:

1. **This overview**: what the process looks like from start to finish.
2. **[Preparing the photos](preparing-photos.md)**: what makes a source photo usable, and how to ask relatives for one.
3. **[Choosing a scene](choosing-a-scene.md)**: matching the setting to the occasion and the size of the group.

## The process in one paragraph

You collect one clear photo of each person (and each pet), choose a scene, check the headcount and the canvas shape, confirm you have permission to use everyone's photos, and generate the portrait. Then you check every face and redo the ones that do not look right. The tool used as the example throughout is the [VibePico family photo maker](https://vibepico.com/family-photo-maker), which takes a separate upload for every family member and has room for as many as eight people plus three pets.

## Why separate photos work

Families rarely gather in one place with a photographer. Combining individual photos removes the need for everyone to be in the same room on the same day. It also lets each person choose a photo they like of themselves, which tends to make the final picture more popular with the family than a group shot where someone blinked.

## What it does not fix

Combining photos cannot invent detail that is not in the source. A blurry, dark or sideways photo leads to a face that looks like someone else. That is why most of this guide is about preparation.
